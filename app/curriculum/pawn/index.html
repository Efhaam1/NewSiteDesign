Stage 1 of 5 · Pawn · The Chess World

Everything a child needs to play a legal game.

The first stage takes a child who has never touched a board to one who can set it up, play every piece legally, spot a checkmate and tell it from a stalemate — and then win material on purpose.

  • 41sessions

How the stage runs

Sessions one to seven are the six pieces, one at a time, then a review that puts them together. Unit two adds setup, promotion, captures and piece values, and introduces the first thinking habit — THE LOOK, which is checking whether anything of yours is under attack before you move. Unit three is check, checkmate and stalemate, and it is where the first level ends. Level 1B is the same board used offensively: hanging pieces, counting attackers and defenders, forced mate in one, king and queen against a bare king, two rooks, and finally the fork, the pin and the skewer.

Every session in Pawn Stage

In teaching order, with the idea each lesson is about and its stated objective — both in the curriculum’s own words.

Level 1AThe Chess World

21 sessions · 3 units · sessions 1–21

Unit 1The Board & The Pieces 7 sessions

  1. 1 The Board & The Rook Teaching 50 min · 8 positions Teach it free

    The rook: straight-line movement along files and ranks, and capturing by landing on an enemy piece

    Show every legal rook move and capture from a given square, and explain why a rook can never jump over a piece.

    opens the curriculum

  2. 2 The Bishop Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    The bishop’s diagonal movement and its light/dark-square identity

    Show all legal bishop moves and captures from a given square and state which colour squares a bishop is bound to.

    taught after 1

  3. 3 The Queen Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    The queen as rook + bishop combined; the most powerful piece

    Show all legal queen moves and captures from a given square and explain why the queen is the most powerful piece.

    taught after 1 · 2

  4. 4 The King Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    The king moves one square in any direction; the piece you must protect

    Show all legal king moves and captures (one square in any direction) and explain that two kings can never stand on touching squares.

    taught after 1 · 2 · 3

  5. 5 The Knight Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    The knight’s L-shaped jump; the only piece that leaps over others

    Show all legal knight moves and captures from a given square (the L-shape) and explain that the knight is the only piece that jumps over others.

    taught after 1 · 2 · 3 · 4

  6. 6 The Pawn Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Pawn movement: forward march, two-square first move, diagonal capture

    Show legal pawn moves and captures and explain that a pawn captures differently (diagonally) from how it moves (straight forward).

    taught after 1 · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5

  7. 7 Move Like a Master (Unit Review) Review 50 min · 8 positions

    Review of all six pieces’ movement and captures

    Correctly move and capture with every piece in mixed recognition exercises, identifying which piece can win the material before playing.

    taught after 1 · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6

Unit 2Setting Up & First Skills 6 sessions

  1. 8 The Starting Position Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    Correct board setup (’light on right’, ’queen on her colour’)

    Set up a chessboard correctly from empty every time, using ’light on right’ and ’queen on her colour’, and make a legal first move.

    taught after 1 · 2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6 · 7

  2. 9 Pawn Promotion Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    A pawn reaching the last rank becomes a queen (or other piece)

    Promote a pawn correctly upon reaching the last rank and explain why promotion makes pawns valuable, including promoting by capturing.

    taught after 3 · 6 · 8

  3. 10 Capturing & Defending Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    How captures work; noticing when a piece is attacked

    Capture an undefended enemy piece and recognise when one of your own pieces is under attack.

    taught after 6 · 7 · 9

  4. 11 Piece Values Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    Relative piece values (P1, N3, B3, R5, Q9) and counting material

    State each piece’s point value and decide whether a trade wins, loses, or is equal in material.

    taught after 3 · 10

  5. 12 Is My Piece Safe? (THE LOOK) Guided practice 50 min · 6 positions

    Introducing THE LOOK: check piece safety before every move

    Apply THE LOOK before each move in a guided game and avoid leaving pieces hanging.

    taught after 10 · 11

  6. 13 Unit Review: Safe & Smart Review 50 min · 8 positions

    Review of setup, promotion, captures, values, and THE LOOK

    Demonstrate safe captures and trades while applying THE LOOK across mixed review positions.

    taught after 8 · 9 · 10 · 11 · 12

Unit 3Check & Checkmate 8 sessions

  1. 14 Check! Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    Check: attacking the enemy king

    Recognise when a king is in check and identify the checking piece, and deliver a check with any piece.

    taught after 10 · 11 · 12

  2. 15 Escaping Check Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    The three ways out of check: move, block, capture

    Find every legal way to escape a given check (move the king, block the check, or capture the checker).

    taught after 14

  3. 16 Checkmate! Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    Checkmate: a check with no escape — the goal of chess

    Explain why a position is checkmate by showing the king has no legal escape, and deliver checkmate in one move.

    taught after 14 · 15

  4. 17 Is This Checkmate? Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    Distinguishing check, checkmate, and ’not mate’ (escape exists)

    Correctly classify positions as check, checkmate, or safe, and choose the move that is truly checkmate rather than only a check.

    taught after 14 · 15 · 16

  5. 18 Stalemate — The Sneaky Draw Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    Stalemate: no legal move and NOT in check = draw

    Distinguish stalemate from checkmate and choose a mating move instead of accidentally stalemating.

    taught after 16 · 17

  6. 19 Castling & Special Moves Teaching 50 min · 8 positions

    Castling (moving the king to safety while activating a rook, in a single move) and the special pawn capture en passant — the last two rules a beginner needs to play a full, legal game

    Castle king-side and queen-side, state the four conditions that allow castling.

    taught after 4 · 8 · 14 · 16

  7. 20 Check, Mate, or Stalemate? (Unit Review) Review 50 min · 8 positions

    Review distinguishing check / checkmate / stalemate

    Sort mixed positions into check, checkmate, or stalemate accurately, and deliver checkmate without stalemating.

    taught after 14 · 15 · 16 · 17 · 18

  8. 21 Level 1A Checkpoint: Board Explorer Checkpoint 55 min · 8 positions

    Assessment of all Level 1A skills (movement, setup, values, check/mate/stalemate, THE LOOK)

    Demonstrate Level 1A mastery on novel positions: piece movement, captures, values, check, checkmate, and stalemate awareness.

    taught after 7 · 13 · 20

Level 1BFirst Battles

20 sessions · 3 units · sessions 22–41

Unit 4Winning Material 6 sessions

  1. 22 Hanging Pieces Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Spotting undefended (hanging) pieces — yours and the opponent’s

    Identify hanging (undefended and attacked) pieces in a position and capture a free piece, while keeping your own pieces defended.

    taught after 10 · 11 · 12 · 21

  2. 23 Defending Your Pieces Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Ways to save an attacked piece: move, defend, block, counterattack

    Save an attacked piece using the most appropriate of the defensive methods: move, defend, block, capture the attacker, or counterattack.

    taught after 22

  3. 24 Counting Attackers & Defenders Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Deciding if a capture is safe by counting attackers vs defenders

    Determine whether capturing on a square wins or loses material by counting attackers and defenders and comparing values.

    taught after 22 · 23

  4. 25 The Double Attack Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Attacking two targets at once (gateway to the fork)

    Create a double attack that wins material because the opponent cannot save both targets.

    taught after 22 · 23 · 24

  5. 26 Win a Piece! (Guided Practice) Guided practice 55 min · 6 positions

    Applying material-winning ideas in guided games

    Win material in a guided game by spotting hanging pieces and creating double attacks.

    taught after 22 · 23 · 24 · 25

  6. 27 Unit Review: Material Master Review 50 min · 8 positions

    Review of hanging pieces, defending, counting, double attack

    Win or save material across mixed review positions by choosing the right material-winning or defensive idea.

    taught after 22 · 23 · 24 · 25 · 26

Unit 5Checkmate Skills 6 sessions

  1. 28 Checkmate in One Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Delivering forced checkmate in a single move

    Find the checkmate-in-one in a variety of positions, confirming the king has no escape.

    taught after 16 · 17 · 20

  2. 29 King & Queen Mate — Driving to the Edge Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    K+Q vs K technique part 1: pushing the lone king to the edge

    Use the queen and king together to drive a lone king to the edge of the board.

    taught after 16 · 18 · 28

  3. 30 King & Queen Mate — The Finish (Avoid Stalemate!) Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    K+Q vs K technique part 2: delivering mate without stalemating

    Deliver checkmate with K+Q vs K once the king is on the edge, while avoiding stalemate.

    taught after 18 · 28 · 29

  4. 31 The Back-Rank Mate Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Checkmating a castled king trapped behind its own pawns

    Deliver a back-rank checkmate and recognise back-rank weaknesses, including making luft to defend.

    taught after 16 · 28

  5. 32 The Two-Rook (Lawnmower) Mate Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Checkmating with two rooks using the rolling/ladder method

    Checkmate a lone king with two rooks using the lawnmower (ladder) technique.

    taught after 16 · 28

  6. 33 Finish the Game! (Unit Review) Review 50 min · 8 positions

    Review of mate-in-one and basic checkmating techniques

    Deliver checkmate in mixed K+Q, K+R-style, two-rook, and back-rank positions, choosing the right technique.

    taught after 28 · 29 · 30 · 31 · 32

Unit 6First Tactics & Thinking 8 sessions

  1. 34 The Fork (Knight Fork) Teaching 60 min · 8 positions

    The knight fork (a double attack delivered by the knight)

    Execute a knight fork that wins material in simple positions, including the royal fork of king and queen.

    taught after 5 · 14 · 22 · 25 · 33

  2. 35 The Fork Family Teaching 60 min · 8 positions

    Forks made by the queen, rook, bishop, and pawn (the whole fork family, including the royal fork)

    Find and play forks with the queen, rook, bishop, and pawn that win material, and recognise a royal fork delivered by a piece other than the knight.

    taught after 5 · 10 · 11 · 22 · 25 · 34

  3. 36 The Pin Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    The pin — freezing a piece in front of a more valuable one

    Identify a pin and use it to win or immobilise a piece.

    taught after 25 · 34 · 35

  4. 37 The Skewer Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    The skewer — the pin’s twin, attacking the more valuable piece first

    Execute a skewer that wins material and distinguish it from a pin.

    taught after 35 · 36

  5. 38 Checks, Captures, Threats (THE SCAN) Teaching 55 min · 8 positions

    Introducing THE SCAN — systematically scanning checks, captures, threats

    List the checks, captures, and threats in a position before choosing a move, and use THE SCAN to find a winning tactic.

    taught after 34 · 35 · 36 · 37

  6. 39 Think Then Move (Guided Practice) Guided practice 55 min · 6 positions

    Applying THE SCAN in tournament-style guided games

    Use THE SCAN before each move in a guided game to find tactics and stay safe.

    taught after 34 · 35 · 36 · 37 · 38

  7. 40 Stage 1 Comprehensive Review Review 55 min · 8 positions

    Interleaved review of all Stage 1 tactics, mates, and thinking habits

    Solve mixed Stage 1 problems, identifying the right idea (movement, material, mate, or tactic) before solving.

    taught after 7 · 13 · 20 · 27 · 33 · 38

  8. 41 Pawn Stage Final Assessment & Graduation Checkpoint 60 min · 8 positions

    Stage 1 final assessment on novel positions; promotion to Knight Stage

    Demonstrate Pawn Stage mastery on novel positions to graduate to the Knight Stage.

    taught after 21 · 33 · 38 · 40
