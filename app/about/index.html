About

Efhaam means “to cause someone to understand”.

Which is the product in one word. What an academy licenses here is not chess content — there is plenty of that, free, everywhere. It is a coach’s ability to make a room understand something, written down 213 times.

A word, and the whole thesis

Efhaam is said ef-HAAM. It is the Arabic إفهام, the causative form of the root ف-ه-م — fahm, understanding. The causative is the whole point: not to understand, but to cause someone else to understand. That is a description of teaching, and it is the founder’s own given name.

English readers tend to read it as “ef-HAM” on first sight. That was known when the name was chosen and accepted rather than solved — the pronunciation is set once, in the footer of every page, and never inside the logo.

The name also carries no category, which is deliberate: every headline and every card on this site says chess curriculum for academies beside it, because the word will not do that job.

the name

It started with a syllabus that said what to teach and never how

The project began as an audit of a real academy’s written syllabus — a ninety-eight-session document, genuinely thought about, used by real trainers. It listed topics in a sensible order. It contained almost nothing a trainer could stand in front of a room and use.

That is the ordinary state of chess teaching material, and it is why every academy quietly depends on its best coaches. A topic list is not an hour. Somebody still has to decide the warm-up, find the positions, write the questions, build an activity, set the homework and guess how long any of it takes — every week, per group, forever. When that work happens in one person’s head, the academy has no standard. It has staff.

So the unit of this curriculum is not a topic. It is an hour: ordered, timed where timing is honest, with the positions chosen, the questions written and the homework set, in every one of the 213.

The syllabus gives trainers what to teach but never how to teach it.
The audit finding, 2026

The academy is not named.That audit was internal analysis of somebody else’s material, and publishing a critical review of an identifiable third party is not something this site is going to do. The finding is the part that matters, and the finding is general.

What Efhaam is, and what it is not

It is

  • A written curriculum, licensed to an academy. One licence is the whole thing; nothing above the entry tier adds a lesson.
  • A teaching standard. The same eight-part hour, the same graded positions and the same stated outcomes in every coach’s hands.
  • For ages 5–14. Five stages, from naming the squares to a graduation paper.
  • Something a coach reads, not something that teaches. Every session is written for a competent trainer rather than an exceptional one.

It is not

  • A place to play chess. There is no engine, no ladder and no opponent.
  • A children’s app. Children never log in. The product is for the adult standing at the board.
  • A content library. A library is a pile you search. This is 213 sessions in one order, each naming the ones before it.
  • A replacement for a coach. It removes the invention, not the teaching.

The authority here is the structure, not a byline

This page is not going to tell you a famous player wrote the curriculum, because that is not what happened and you could not check it if it were. What can be checked is how the 213 sessions are built, and it is the same five things every time.

  1. 01
    One schemaEvery session is the same document: the same fields in the same places, filled in for a different hour. Not a folder of documents that happen to be about chess.
  2. 02
    One shapeThe same eight parts in the same order, 1,701 of them written across the curriculum — a warm-up, an introduction, an explanation, a discussion, guided practice, positions, an activity, homework. A coach who has taught one session knows the architecture of every other one.
  3. 03
    One orderPrerequisites on every session but the first, four on the median and 11 at the deepest, with no forward references anywhere in the 213. 30 units, each with a review inside it; 10 levels, each closing in a graded session.
  4. 04
    One habit per stageFive thinking routines ladder across the five stages — THE LOOK, THE SCAN, THE PLAN, THE CALCULATE, THE EVALUATE — so a child is taught a way of looking at a position, not only a list of patterns.
  5. 05
    Checked by machine, not by eyeEvery puzzle position is validated for chess legality: the position legal, the side to move correct, and every solution line playable to its stated end. 4,751 checks, 0 errors.

And precisely what that check proves.Chess legality, on the puzzle positions. It does not mean the curriculum passes every internal check it has, because it does not: the same tool reports formatting problems in the session files that this site’s figures do not depend on, and they are being worked through. It also does not mean every position in the bundle is covered — the check covers the puzzle positions. “Every puzzle position checked for legality” is the exact claim, and it is the only one made.

Six things you will not find on this site

A product with no customers yet has one thing it can offer instead of proof: an accurate account of itself. This is that account, and it is enforced — the rule in this repository is that a figure which cannot be counted gets removed rather than softened.

  1. 01No testimonials, and no logos

    When academies are teaching from this, their words will appear with their names on them. Until then there is nothing to quote, so nothing is quoted. The same goes for case studies, review stars and customer counts.

  2. 02No promise about a rating

    Each stage records the strength a student at that stage usually plays at. That is a correlation, published as one. No page here says a child will reach a number, and no page ever will.

  3. 03No claims about intelligence or school results

    The research on chess and general cognitive gains is contested and its own authors say so. A chess curriculum is a chess curriculum.

  4. 04No figure that cannot be counted

    Every number on this site is read at build time out of the curriculum bundle — 213 sessions, 1,640 positions, 1,247 questions. If a figure could not be counted it was removed rather than adjusted.

  5. 05No feature described as built that is not

    Printed packs, an owner dashboard, parent reports, exports and single sign-on are not built. They are named as unbuilt on the licence schedule, on the coach page and on the academy page, in the same words.

  6. 06No child accounts and no child emails

    Not a roadmap item and not a compliance posture to be revisited: children do not have logins here, and the product asks for nothing about them. Ever.
